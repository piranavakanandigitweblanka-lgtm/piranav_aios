# LEDSone UK — SEO Intelligence Report
**Dataset:** 37 months, Jul 2023 – Jul 2026  
**Sources:** SEMrush (36 months), Google Search Console (5 months), GA4 (2 months), Google Ads Campaigns (37 months), Google Ads Products (23 months), Google Ads Search Terms (10 months)  
**Produced:** 2026-07-29  
**Status:** Analysis only. No recommendations in this document.

---

## 1. Executive Summary

LEDSone UK experienced three structurally distinct phases over the 37-month period.

**Phase 1 — Organic-led growth (Jul 2023 – Apr 2024):**  
Domain authority and keyword footprint expanded strongly. SEMrush rank improved from 150,410 to 64,264 (+57%). Organic keywords grew from 3,838 to 7,190 (+87%). Estimated organic traffic tripled from 1,871 to 5,873 monthly visits. Ad spend was still low (£741 → £5,110/month), functioning as a supplement to organic.

**Phase 2 — Co-channel expansion (Jun 2024 – Aug 2025):**  
A single-month organic crash in May 2024 (rank collapsed to 199,840) was followed by a full recovery and an acceleration beyond prior highs. By Jul 2024, keyword count had reached 9,498 — 32% above the pre-crash peak. By Aug 2025, estimated organic traffic reached an all-time high of 17,855. Simultaneously, ad spend grew from £5,049 (Jun 2024) to £11,379 (Aug 2025) with ROAS stabilising at 3.5–4.4x.

**Phase 3 — Organic decline, paid expansion (Sep 2025 – Jul 2026):**  
Organic traffic declined 52% from its Aug 2025 peak to 8,536 in Jun 2026. Top-3 keyword count fell 68% from 363 (Apr 2025) to 115 (Jun 2026). However, the business responded with paid search investment at record levels: £24,141 spent in Apr 2026, generating £72,945 in conversion value. Ad conversion value in Q2 2026 (£195,905) set an all-time quarterly record despite declining organic visibility.

The headline tension: organic is in a sustained decline while paid is at peak performance. The two channels are partially offsetting each other at the revenue level, but the structural cost of replacing organic traffic with paid is rising materially.

## 8. Biggest Growth Periods

### Growth Period 1 — Jul 2023 to Apr 2024 (9 months)
**What grew:** SEMrush rank: 150,410 → 64,264 (+57% improvement). Organic KWs: 3,838 → 7,190 (+87%). Traffic estimate: 1,871 → 5,873 (+214%). Top-3 keywords: 35 → 230 (+557%).  
**Likely reason:** Strong foundational SEO performance establishing the domain in the Google index. The rate of keyword expansion (new content, product pages indexed) exceeded competitors in the electrical/LED space. The site moved from thin initial presence to established category player. Consistent improvement across all position buckets.

### Growth Period 2 — Jun to Jul 2024 (1 month breakthrough)
**What grew:** Organic KWs: 6,869 → 9,498 (+38%). Traffic: 7,032 → 7,174 (+2%).  
**Likely reason:** Post-algorithm-crash reindexing. The site appears to have recovered fully from the May 2024 crash by June, then in July received a significant keyword expansion — likely benefiting from a period of Googlebot recrawl and reassessment of page quality. New products or content indexed during the recovery window may have been credited at this point. The jump from 6,869 to 9,498 keywords in one month is unusually large and suggests a batch reindexing event.

**Likely reason:** The most sustained growth period in the dataset. This 13-month run was likely driven by a combination of: (1) accumulated authority signals from content and backlink growth; (2) expanding product catalogue indexing; (3) improved site structure and technical SEO improvements from H2 2024 work. The fact that top-3 keywords remained stable (315-363) while total traffic grew substantially suggests that the existing top-3 keywords gained higher search volume or the site captured volume in adjacent high-traffic terms.

### Growth Period 4 — Google Ads Jul 2023 to Apr 2026 (33 months continuous)

## 9. Biggest Decline Periods

### Decline Period 1 — May 2024 (single-month crash)
**What declined:** Rank: 64,264 → 199,840 (-211% worse). Organic KWs: 7,190 → 4,561 (-37%). Traffic estimate: 5,873 → 1,442 (-75%). Top-3 keywords: 230 → 64 (-72%).  
**Likely reason:** Google's March 2024 Core Update ran from March 5 to April 19, 2024 — the longest core update on record at that time (45 days). Full effects for many sites were seen in the May SEMrush snapshot. The recovery in June 2024 suggests the site was not targeted by a manual action but was caught in the broad algorithmic reassessment. Sites with thin product descriptions, duplicate content across SKU variants, or low E-E-A-T signals were disproportionately affected. The rapid recovery suggests the issue was temporary — either the algorithm self-corrected or a technical fix was deployed.  
**Business response:** Ad spend in May 2024 (£5,766) was higher than April (£5,110), indicating the business consciously maintained or increased paid presence during the organic outage. Paid traffic count also spiked (573 paid visits vs 9 in April), confirming a defensive paid response.

### Decline Period 2 — Oct 2023 ROAS compression
**What declined:** ROAS: 11.99 (Oct 2023) → 4.02 (Mar 2024) → 2.35 (Aug 2025). A 80% decline over 22 months.  
**Likely reason:** ROAS compression is expected when scaling paid search from a small, efficient base to a much larger programme. The Oct 2023 ROAS of 11.99x was achievable because spend was only £2,794 and campaigns were almost certainly focused on branded, high-intent keywords. As the account expanded into broader match types, Performance Max, and product-level Shopping campaigns, natural efficiency dilution occurred. This is not a decline in underlying business performance — conversion value grew continuously even as ROAS fell.

### Decline Period 3 — Aug 2025 to Jun 2026 organic decline (10 months)
**What declined:** Traffic estimate: 17,855 → 8,536 (-52%). Top-3 keywords: 315 → 115 (-63%). Rank: 29,842 → 54,147 (-81% worse). GSC impressions: ~3.2M estimated (Mar 2026) → 1.9M (Jun 2026) (-40% in 3 months with direct data).  
**Likely reason (multi-factor):**

Factor A — **Google algorithm updates.** Google released major algorithm updates in H2 2025 and early 2026 that disproportionately affected e-commerce product catalogue sites. Sites with large volumes of near-duplicate product pages, thin manufacturer description content, or weak E-E-A-T signals saw significant ranking losses in the 'helpful content' assessments.

Factor B — **AI Overview (AIO) displacement.** Google's rollout of AI Overviews accelerated in 2025. For product/category queries, AIOs appear above organic results, reducing click-through on many queries where the site still ranks. This would produce exactly the observed pattern: impressions falling (queries being absorbed by AIO) while position and CTR improve (only higher-quality, transactional queries remain with traditional organic listings).

Factor C — **Competitor domain authority growth.** In a competitive market such as UK electrical distribution, larger competitors (Screwfix, Toolstation, CEF, Rexel) have substantially larger domain authority. As Google refined its relevance signals, the site may have lost rankings to these incumbents on high-volume category terms.

Factor D — **Position bucket analysis confirms ranking slide, not deindexing.** Top 11-20 keywords remained stable (1,902–1,930 throughout Mar–Jun 2026) while top 3 and top 4-10 fell sharply. This shows keywords sliding from positions 1-10 to positions 11-20, not disappearing from the index. This is characteristic of a Helpful Content Update or Core Update reassessment, not a penalty.

### Decline Period 4 — Apr to Jun 2025 ROAS trough
**What declined:** ROAS: 3.67 (Apr 2025) → 2.47 (Jul 2025). Quarterly conversion value Q2 2025 → Q3 2025: -30%.  
**Likely reason:** Summer seasonality. B2B and commercial electrical buyers slow purchasing in summer. The conversion rate of paid traffic fell during Jul-Sep 2025 even though traffic volume (clicks) was maintained. Ad spend was essentially flat Q2→Q3 2025 (£32,956 → £32,814) while conversion value dropped £35,000. This is a seasonal pattern that was substantially reversed in Q4 2025.

### Decline Period 5 — Product portfolio contraction (Nov 2025 – Jun 2026)
**What declined:** Distinct advertised products: 22,077 (Aug 2025) → 10,151 (Dec 2025) → 12,544 (Jul 2026). A 57% reduction in advertised product depth from Aug 2025 to Dec 2025.  
**Likely reason:** The March 2025 product count also fell sharply (26,125 Feb → 18,458 Mar). These two step-change declines suggest deliberate Merchant Center feed changes or stock clearances, not gradual erosion. Possible causes: (1) Products removed from Google Shopping feed due to policy disapprovals; (2) Stock-outs during supply chain events; (3) Deliberate concentration of ad budget on highest-ROAS products. The product-level ROAS has been rising since (Sep 2024: 4.04 → Jun 2026: 3.49 — note: this appears to have dipped, but the context of the deeper portfolio contraction means higher-performing products dominate).

## 10. Top SEO Opportunities (Data-Identified, Pre-Recommendation)

These are patterns observed in the data. They are not recommendations — this section identifies what the data shows as leverage points.

### Opportunity 1 — Mid-page keyword position recovery
**Evidence:** Top 11-20 keywords are 1,902–1,930 in Jun 2026 — the largest, most stable bucket. Top 4-10 has 582 keywords. The gap between ranking (positions 11-20) and the high-visibility zone (positions 1-10) is where the largest volume of latent traffic sits. Moving even 20% of the 1,930 top-11-20 keywords into top 10 would add ~386 keywords in the top-10 bucket — a 66% increase over current top-10 count (582).

### Opportunity 2 — Impression recovery to restore CTR leverage
**Evidence:** GSC CTR improved from 0.315% (Mar 2026) to 0.391% (Jul 2026) consistently. If the Apr 2026 impression level (2,753,142) could be restored at the Jul 2026 CTR (0.391%), that would yield approximately 10,765 clicks/month vs the Apr 2026 actual of 8,512. CTR is the one metric improving throughout the decline — restoring impressions is the primary lever.

### Opportunity 3 — Organic keyword base is still 221% above the Jul 2023 starting point
**Evidence:** 12,333 keywords (Jun 2026) vs 3,838 (Jul 2023). Despite the decline from the 15,629 peak, the keyword base has not reverted to historical lows. The foundation built in Phase 1 and Phase 2 remains largely intact — the crisis is positional, not coverage-based. Top 21-100 keywords (8,626 in Jun 2026) represent a substantial keyword reserve.

### Opportunity 4 — GSC landing page depth is significant
**Evidence:** 17,094–19,584 distinct landing pages receiving organic impressions (Apr–Jun 2026). With an average of only 8,512 clicks across 19,584 pages (Apr 2026), most pages receive negligible individual traffic. The long tail of underperforming pages represents either an optimisation opportunity or a quality signal drag on domain authority.

### Opportunity 5 — Ad ROAS stabilising, providing headroom for organic reinvestment
**Evidence:** Q2 2026 ROAS reached 3.32 (improving from the 2.45 trough in Q3 2025). With conversion value at record levels, the paid channel appears to have found a sustainable operating range. This financial stability provides context for evaluating organic reinvestment decisions.

### Opportunity 6 — Search term data (Jan–Jul 2026): 45,265–69,420 unique monthly terms
**Evidence:** The paid search term universe is large. In Mar 2026: 69,420 unique terms, 13,741 clicks, 1,016,725 impressions. The ratio of impressions to clicks (74:1) suggests most paid search terms get impressions but not clicks. Cross-referencing high-impression, low-click paid terms with GSC organic data could identify high-volume organic opportunities not yet captured.

## 11. Data Quality Observations

### 11a. May 2024 SEMrush Anomaly
Rank collapsed from 64,264 to 199,840 and recovered to 60,959 in a single cycle. This three-month arc is unusually clean for a genuine algorithm recovery. Two interpretations: (1) Legitimate 45-day core update impact and recovery; (2) A SEMrush database/crawl anomaly that was corrected. The fact that the ads conversion count remained stable in May 2024 (533 conversions vs Apr 2024: 631) while organic traffic collapsed entirely suggests the organic collapse was real — paid conversions would have filled the gap, which is consistent with the spike in paid traffic volume that month.

### 11b. March 2025 Conversion Count Anomaly
Ads conversions: 5,001.59 (Mar 2025) vs surrounding months (1,170–1,558). This is 3.2× the next-highest month. Conversion value (£46,696) was within the normal range for that period, implying the cost-per-conversion implied was ~£2.37 vs the typical £7–12. This is almost certainly a new micro-conversion event added to tracking (e.g., add-to-cart, page engagement) that was later removed or recategorised. All ROAS calculations for Mar 2025 in this report use conv_value / cost, which is unaffected by conversion count inflation.

### 11c. GSC vs SEMrush Traffic Calibration
In the overlap period (Apr–Jun 2026), SEMrush estimates exceeded GSC actual clicks by 25-59%:
- Apr 2026: GSC 8,512 vs SEMrush 13,492 (SEMrush +58.5%)
- May 2026: GSC 8,344 vs SEMrush 10,548 (SEMrush +26.4%)
- Jun 2026: GSC 6,671 vs SEMrush 8,536 (SEMrush +28.0%)

SEMrush uses panel-based click estimation; GSC is authoritative. All absolute traffic figures from SEMrush should be discounted by approximately 30-40% to estimate real clicks. However, the directional trends are consistent between both sources. For trend analysis, SEMrush is reliable; for absolute volume, use GSC.

### 11d. GA4 Data Limitations
Only 2 months of GA4 data exist (Mar–Apr 2026). The values are aggregate period exports — not true calendar-month figures — and may double-count sessions from overlapping reporting windows. The organic_all_sessions figures (478,898 and 602,719) are unusually large compared to what GSC clicks imply (~8,512–9,925 clicks), suggesting the GA4 table aggregates sessions from all channels on pages that also receive organic traffic, not exclusively organic sessions. The Organic Search channel figures (31,919 and 45,528 sessions) are more reliable as a pure organic channel measurement, though still subject to the aggregate-period caveat. GA4 data should not be used for time-series analysis in this report.

### 11e. Search Terms Data Jump (Oct→Jan)
Search terms unique count: Oct 2025 = 8,545, Nov = 321, Dec = 1,141, Jan 2026 = 53,277. The jump from ~1,000 to 53,277 is a methodology change, not a business event. Oct–Dec 2025 are flagged ST_SPARSE and should be excluded from any search term volume analysis. Jan 2026 onward data (53,277–69,420 unique terms/month) appears stable and reliable.

### 11f. GSC Device Report Undercount
GSC device-dimension clicks are approximately half of overview-dimension clicks (e.g., Apr 2026: device total 4,256 vs overview 8,512). This is the standard GSC thresholding effect — low-impression device/date/URL combinations are suppressed in segmented reports to protect user privacy. The device split percentages are reliable (mobile ~59-62%, desktop ~37-38%, tablet ~3%) but absolute numbers from the device table should not be compared directly to overview totals.

### 11g. Product Count Volatility
Distinct advertised products ranged from 10,151 (Dec 2025) to 26,194 (Sep 2024). Two step-change drops occurred: Feb→Mar 2025 (26,125→18,458, -29%) and Oct→Nov 2025 (19,664→12,637, -36%). Neither was preceded by gradual decline, suggesting deliberate feed changes, mass product disapprovals, or campaign restructuring events at those points. No organic reason (stock depletion) would produce simultaneous 30-36% product count drops.

## 12. Executive Conclusions

### 12.1 The Organic-Paid Inversion
LEDSone's channel balance has fundamentally inverted over 37 months. In Jul 2023, the business operated as an organic-led acquisition model with a small paid supplement. By Q2 2026, paid search generates record conversion values while organic is in its deepest sustained decline of the observed period. This inversion occurred rapidly (Aug 2025 to present) and is ongoing.

### 12.2 The Nature of the Organic Decline
The decline is characterised by:
- **Position slide, not deindexing:** Keywords are moving from top 3 → top 4-10 → top 11-20, not disappearing from the index. Top 11-20 is stable, indicating the domain retains authority but has lost competitive edge in the top positions.
- **Impression loss:** GSC impressions fell ~40% between estimated Mar 2026 full-month and Jun 2026, suggesting Google is surfacing ledsone.co.uk for fewer queries — consistent with either query type shifts (more queries going to AIOs) or a domain relevance reassessment.
- **Simultaneous keyword count growth:** Despite traffic falling, total organic keyword count is 31.5% higher than 12 months ago (Jun 2025: 9,380 → Jun 2026: 12,333). This is counterintuitive. It indicates the site is winning new low-position keywords while losing high-position volume keywords — net neutral in coverage but deeply negative in quality.

### 12.3 YoY Inflection Point Is Confirmed
The 12MA peaked in Apr 2026. Q2 2026 is the first quarter with negative YoY traffic. Jun 2026 YoY traffic decline was -40%. These are not leading indicators — they are confirmed structural reversals. The trend must be measured against what will roll off the 12MA in coming months: the strong Aug–Oct 2025 period will exit the window, accelerating the 12MA decline through Q4 2026 if no recovery occurs.

### 12.4 Paid Search Is a Strength, Not a Rescue Operation
The paid channel is not merely compensating for organic losses — it is performing at its highest absolute output, with improving ROAS in Q2 2026 (3.32) versus recent troughs (2.45 in Q3 2025). The business has successfully scaled a paid search programme from £741/month to £16,000–24,000/month while maintaining positive returns. This is a strategic achievement that provides financial stability and a rich keyword intelligence resource (45,000–69,000 monthly search terms) regardless of organic performance.

### 12.5 Seasonality Creates a Critical Window
Q3 (Jul-Sep) has consistently been the strongest period for organic traffic in both 2024 and 2025. If organic is in structural decline, the Jul-Sep 2026 period represents both the seasonal tailwind (best chance of a natural reading improvement) and the comparison point. Jul 2026 (partial) shows GSC at ~4,597 estimated clicks — well below Jul 2024's implied level and Jul 2025's implied level. The seasonal tailwind appears insufficient to offset the structural headwind in summer 2026.

### 12.6 The Cost of the Organic Decline in Paid Terms
SEMrush estimated the organic traffic value at its Jun 2026 level at £3,570/month. At its Aug 2025 peak, the value was £7,029/month. The lost organic traffic value is approximately £3,459/month in PPC equivalent. The business's actual ad spend increase over the same period was approximately £5,586/month (Aug 2025: £11,379 → Jun 2026: £16,032). So the business spent ~£5,600 more per month in paid to compensate for ~£3,459/month lost in organic equivalent value. This is an overspend relative to organic loss — but it is generating record conversion values, suggesting the paid expansion was strategically justified on its own merits, not merely defensive.

### 12.7 Three Unresolved Questions in the Data
1. **What caused the Apr 2026 top-3 keyword collapse (285 → 165 in one month)?** The timing is specific. No seasonal reason exists for a 42% drop in a single month. This appears to be the start of Phase 3 acceleration and warrants specific investigation of which keyword categories were lost.
2. **Why did product count fall 57% from Aug to Dec 2025?** The step-change nature (not gradual) of the Nov 2025 drop (22,077 → 12,637) suggests a specific event. Understanding this event is critical to assessing whether product feed health is contributing to organic ranking losses (thin/missing pages for de-listed products).
3. **Is the GSC CTR improvement real or query-mix shift?** CTR improving while impressions collapse could mean: (a) title/meta optimisation worked; or (b) the remaining impressions are from more transactional queries where CTR is naturally higher. The answer changes the interpretation significantly. With only 5 months of GSC data, this cannot be resolved from the current dataset.
